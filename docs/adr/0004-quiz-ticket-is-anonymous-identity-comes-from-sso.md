# A quiz ticket proves attendance, not identity — the name comes from SSO

The five-minute quiz needs two independent facts about a person: *who they are*, so the answers can be attributed, and *were they in the room*, so the quiz measures the lecture rather than a group chat. The first draft of the requirements tried to get both from one artefact — print one QR per student with that student's name behind it — and then separately said the name would come from authentik SSO. Both cannot be true at once: if SSO already supplies the name, a per-student code carries no information, and the requirement to log "both the student name and the code they logged in with" becomes a requirement to log the same fact twice.

A ticket is therefore anonymous. Roughly as many tickets as there are seats are printed, handed out in the room to whoever is sitting there, and each carries a random one-time token and nothing else. Scanning it starts the OIDC round trip; the name arrives in `preferred_username` from authentik exactly as it does for JupyterHub (see `msu/infra`, role `jupyterhub`), and the ticket is burned and bound to that name. The pair is what gets logged, and the two halves now mean different things: the name says who answered, the ticket says they were physically present when the paper was handed out.

The practical payoff is that nothing has to be right in advance. A named ticket has to reach the correct pair of hands, which means a correct group roster the day before, a sorting problem at the door, and a broken quiz for anyone who swapped seats or is not on the list yet — and on 1 September the roster is exactly the thing the faculty does not have, because my.msu accounts are issued after student cards. Anonymous tickets are interchangeable: a stack of paper, a leftover pile, and a visitor who is not on any list can still take the quiz.

The cost is that a ticket cannot select the questions, since at print time nobody knows whose it is. That is why the per-student set is derived at login from (lecture, login) instead of being generated in advance — the two decisions are the same decision seen from two sides.

What this does not do: it does not stop someone from taking a photo of the QR and sending it out of the room. Nothing printed on paper can. It raises the cost from zero to "find an accomplice with an unused ticket", which for a five-minute formative quiz is the right amount of effort to spend.
